// 01. IDENTITY & CODE OF ENGINEERING

Bridging Pure Silicon to Autonomous Systems

An electronics engineering undergraduate at RGUKT Ongole with practical industry internship experience, national robotics standing, and an obsession with deterministic hardware-software co-design.

Who I Am & How I Build

I approach engineering as an unbroken system-development cycle: understand the requirement, deconstruct the silicon architecture, design the schematics, implement bare-metal or RTOS firmware, measure behavior with oscilloscopes, and rigorously validate the result.

My strongest positioning is an Embedded Firmware Engineer with practical hardware validation, board bring-up, and autonomous robotics experience. Whether configuring timer registers, streaming ADC samples via EasyDMA, or tuning motor PID loops for national robotics arenas, I enjoy getting as close to the bare metal as possible.

Beyond microcontrollers, I actively explore VLSI & SoC design, agricultural automation, and marine robotics. I believe good engineering is not just about writing clean code—it is about building physical systems that operate reliably in the real world.

The Five Engineering Principles I Live By

PRINCIPLE 01

Understand Before Implementing

Deconstruct hardware datasheets and peripheral registers rather than relying blindly on third-party black-box libraries.

PRINCIPLE 02

Connect Hardware & Software

Treat firmware, board traces, analog noise, sensors, and mechanical actuation as one tightly coupled unified system.

PRINCIPLE 03

Test What You Build

Use digital storage oscilloscopes, logic analyzers, and automated test harnesses as first-class citizens of daily development.

PRINCIPLE 04

Learn By Building

Explore ideas through physical prototypes, real-world breadboards, custom PCBs, and iterative failure analysis.

PRINCIPLE 05

Design for the Real World

Prioritize determinism, fault recovery, watchdogs, and safety in rehabilitation, field robotics, and industrial applications.
